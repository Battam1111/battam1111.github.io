# Visual Research — Group 2: Design-Conscious Scholars / Writers / Gardeners

Five high-craft personal sites by writers, designers, and digital gardeners. Not ML researchers — but their `personality without cringe` calibration is the exact gap in al-folio. Each entry filters for what is portable to a PhD academic homepage.

Scoring legend (visual craft only, ignoring content): 1–10.

---

## 1. Maggie Appleton — https://maggieappleton.com/

**Visual craft: 9.5/10**

- **Typography.** Serif-led pairing: display + body in a warm, slightly-bookish serif (Tiempos/Fraunces-class) paired with a clean humanist sans for UI/nav. Body line-height is generous (~1.6–1.75). Headings carry weight via size, not bolding — a deliberately editorial register. Drop-caps and italic pull quotes appear in essay pages.
- **Colors.** Cream/parchment background (off-white, slightly warm — ~#FAF7F0-ish range), deep-ink near-black text, single botanical accent: a desaturated forest/sage green for links, leaf icons, and underline hovers. Total palette ≈ 4 colors. Warm-toned overall. No pure white, no pure black.
- **Layout.** Single centered column ~700px for prose, but essay pages widen to a multi-column layout with a margin-note rail for sidenotes. Heavy whitespace. Asymmetric on essay landing where illustrations bleed left.
- **Hero.** Big serif statement (`Maggie makes visual essays...`) — no portrait, no carousel. The first impression is editorial: a magazine, not a portfolio.
- **Micro-craft.** Custom hand-drawn botanical SVG illustrations (vines, leaves, mushrooms, sprouts) functioning as section markers and essay covers. Hover on essay cards lifts subtly with a green underline draw-in. Footnote/sidenote system is the signature. Custom bullet glyphs (leaf, sprout) signal `epistemic status` (seedling/budding/evergreen).
- **Personality without cringe.** The genius is that the illustrations are *her own work, drawn in a consistent ink style* — they are not stock playfulness. Whimsy gets earned because every botanical comes from the same hand at the same line weight. The cream background + serif body reads `quiet scholarship`, then the green sprout in the corner adds warmth without breaking the register.
- **3 things portable to a PhD site:**
  1. **Cream background + dark-warm text + one accent color** (replace al-folio's pure white).
  2. **Editorial serif for body / sans for UI** mixing — kills the `template` smell instantly.
  3. **One signature graphic element** repeated quietly (could be a single ink-style logo mark, not 50 illustrations).

## 2. Andy Matuschak — https://andymatuschak.org/

**Visual craft: 8/10 (restraint discipline)**

- **Typography.** Sans-serif (system stack — looks like SF Pro / system-ui). Single family throughout. Hierarchy via *size and color only*, not weight gymnastics. Body around 18–20px, line-height ~1.5. Wide line measure ~70 chars.
- **Colors.** Near-white background, near-black body text, mid-gray for secondary metadata, single muted blue for links (not the default browser blue — a more subdued slate-blue). ~4 colors. Cool-neutral, fully desaturated.
- **Layout.** Single left-aligned column, generous margins (≈25% gutter each side at desktop). Sections separated by ~80–120px vertical air. List-driven IA — projects as nested indented lists rather than card grids.
- **Hero.** Pure text. Three tiny header links (Email/X/Bsky), name, one descriptive sentence. No portrait, no hero image, no CTA. The most disciplined opening in the set.
- **Micro-craft.** Almost none — and that is the point. Only ornamentation: small gray padlock SVGs marking patron-only items. Links are color-only (no underline). The famous craft is on his evergreen notes pages: stretchtext, floating margin-note cards on click, transclusion previews. The homepage itself is monastic.
- **Personality without cringe.** Zero decoration *is* the personality — it signals `I trust my prose to do the work`. Warmth comes from generous whitespace and the conversational sentence tone, not visual flourish. This is the `brutalism done well` archetype.
- **3 things portable to a PhD site:**
  1. **Nested indented list** as the projects/publications layout (more scholarly than al-folio's card grid).
  2. **No underlined links** — color-only differentiation, but pick a non-default accent (slate-blue, not `#0066cc`).
  3. **One-sentence identity line** under the name — radically more elegant than al-folio's bio paragraph.

## 3. Devon Zuegel — https://devonzuegel.com/

**Visual craft: 7/10**

- **Typography.** Sans-serif single-family, system or near-system. Title weight contrast moderate. Body 16–18px. No font mixing. Restrained, blog-feed register.
- **Colors.** Black on white, single link accent (blue-leaning). 2–3 colors. The least chromatic site in this group — almost monochrome.
- **Layout.** Single left-aligned column. Reverse-chronological stack. Implicit grid via spacing rather than rules/cards. Generous whitespace between entries. Tag-cloud / topic list visible.
- **Hero.** Text-only: name + minimal nav. No portrait. Editorial blog opening, no `landing page` posture.
- **Micro-craft.** Minimal animation surface. Hover states presumably color-only. No custom dividers, no illustrations.
- **Personality without cringe.** Personality emerges entirely from *curatorial range* — the topic mix visible in tags (cities, IVF, Argentina, pirates) signals an unusual mind, but the visual carrier is utterly neutral. The site refuses to perform personality so the content has to. This is the riskiest move in the set: it only works if the writing is strong.
- **3 things portable to a PhD site:**
  1. **Reverse-chronological writing feed** as a homepage section (not buried under `Blog` tab) — communicates active intellectual life.
  2. **Tag-cloud sidebar / footer** showing topical range across a research portfolio.
  3. **Confident under-decoration** — proves that minimalism doesn't require al-folio's templated card system; plain stacked entries can work if the spacing is right.

## 4. Robin Sloan — https://www.robinsloan.com/

**Visual craft: 9/10**

- **Typography.** Serif-primary, classical proportional face (Georgia/Garamond-class) for both display and body. Subtle weight contrast — refuses dramatic extremes. Discretionary hyphenation visible (`pub­lished` with soft hyphen), suggesting actual typographic care. Likely uses CSS `hyphens: auto` + a real serif webfont, not Georgia fallback.
- **Colors.** Black text on white background. A single metallic gold/ochre star (★) used as a curatorial marker on selected entries. 3 colors total. Print-broadsheet register.
- **Layout.** Single centered column, ~15–20% side margins. Vertical rhythm tuned for reading flow, not skimming. Horizontal dividers rendered as three asterisks (`* * *`) — typesetter convention, not `<hr>`.
- **Hero.** Name in large serif + descriptive subtitle. Zero imagery. Closing with `Thanks for stopping by!` microcopy at the bottom — like a printed colophon.
- **Micro-craft.** No animation. Subtle underlined links. Soft-hyphen typesetting. The famous touch: a real **colophon** page with a Latin motto (`Hony soyt qui mal pence`) and an explicit `no tracking` note. The whole site behaves like a printed object, not a web app.
- **Personality without cringe.** Personality is encoded as *typographic seriousness* — the soft hyphens, the `* * *` dividers, the colophon. A reader who knows typography sees the care; a reader who doesn't just sees a clean site. No one feels excluded, no one rolls their eyes. This is the gold-standard `quiet flex`.
- **3 things portable to a PhD site:**
  1. **`* * *` dividers** between sections instead of `<hr>` rules — costs nothing, reads as bookish.
  2. **Soft hyphenation + real serif webfont** for body — the `printed page` register is instantly more authoritative than al-folio's defaults.
  3. **A colophon footer** noting fonts/tools/no-tracking — signals craft without bragging. Massively portable to a PhD site.

## 5. Pirijan (pketh.org) — https://pketh.org/

**Visual craft: 8.5/10**

- **Typography.** Sans-serif (likely system stack), single family, generous line-height. The contrast comes from size and the *yellow* accent on key elements, not weight. Headings unfussy. No serif/handwritten mixing.
- **Colors.** Warm off-white/cream background (not pure white). Near-black text. Signature accent: a saturated yellow used for highlights, tag underlines, the Internet Phonebook references. Small grays for dividers. 4–5 colors. Warm-toned, with one bright pop.
- **Layout.** Single column, narrow measure (~60–70 chars). No sidebar. Reverse-chronological. Mobile-first feel — comfortable margins on phone.
- **Hero.** A small animated GIF (`image.gif`) appears early but is not the dominant element. Tags (`#design`, `#life`, `#biz`) sit subtly above the fold. The Kinopio logo is a quiet sibling link, not a brand banner.
- **Micro-craft.** This is the playful end of the set. Embedded GIFs (waving vegetables, pixel art), hand-traced 1913 cross-stitch graphics, vintage photo references, Kermit-gunslinger animations. Tables are properly styled with alternating rows. Crucially: every image has an attribution credit — the craft ethic is visible.
- **Personality without cringe.** Personality survives the `cute factor` because (a) the cream background and clean typography hold a serious register underneath, (b) the playful elements are *sourced and credited*, signaling thoughtfulness rather than whimsy-for-its-own-sake, and (c) yellow is used very sparingly — one accent, not a rainbow. The same site without the cream background and with white instead would tip into cringe immediately.
- **3 things portable to a PhD site:**
  1. **Warm cream background** (not pure white) — single biggest `cheap` upgrade out of all five sites.
  2. **One saturated accent color used very sparingly** — yellow here, could be deep red or olive for a PhD site. Used for link hover, citation marks, or a single highlight glyph.
  3. **Attribution culture as design** — small credit lines under any image/figure on the site signal scholarly care and also create visual rhythm (small text under big text). Already aligns with academic citation norms.

## Cross-cutting patterns (for synthesis)

- **Background color is the single biggest dial.** 4 of 5 sites use cream/off-white, never pure `#FFFFFF`. Maggie + Pirijan use warm cream; Andy + Devon use near-white; only Robin uses pure white but compensates with a strong serif. *al-folio's pure white background is the cheapest tell that it's a template.*
- **Color palette discipline: 3–5 colors total.** No exceptions. One accent color, used sparingly. Never two competing accents.
- **Serif-led typography correlates with maximum perceived craft.** Maggie (serif), Robin (serif), Pirijan (sans but warm) score highest on `feels designed`. Andy + Devon score lower on visible craft but higher on `discipline`. For a PhD site, **serif body + sans UI** is the highest-leverage move.
- **Hero is text-only across all five.** No portraits, no carousels, no hero images. Name + one descriptive line. al-folio's headshot-first hero is a template smell — keep the headshot but demote it.
- **Personality lives in one signature element, repeated quietly.** Maggie's botanicals, Robin's `* * *` dividers, Pirijan's yellow accent + sourced GIFs. Never decorative spam. **Pick one thing.**
- **What does NOT port to a PhD site:** Maggie-density illustrations (too much hand-craft signal for an academic register), Pirijan-density playful GIFs (reads as `personal blog`, not `researcher`), Devon's near-zero decoration (only works if you have a published book + body of writing visible). What ports: cream background, serif/sans mix, sparing accent, `* * *` dividers, colophon footer, one-sentence identity line, nested-list projects, reverse-chrono writing feed.
