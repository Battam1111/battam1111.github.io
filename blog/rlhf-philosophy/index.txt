13:T61f,<h2 id="-introduction"><a class="anchor" href="#-introduction">🌌 Introduction</a></h2>
<p>Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) is often viewed through a technical lens — as a method to fine-tune large language models (LLMs) to align better with user intent. But under the surface lies a deeper, <strong>philosophical motivation</strong>.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="-the-reward-fallacy"><a class="anchor" href="#-the-reward-fallacy">🔍 The Reward Fallacy</a></h2>
<p>The traditional reward signal is inadequate for capturing human values. It’s brittle, hackable, and often misaligned. The idea that we can <strong>predefine reward functions</strong> for intelligent agents breaks down in the real world.</p>
<blockquote>
<p>“A perfectly rewarded system is not necessarily a morally aligned system.”</p>
</blockquote>
<hr>
<h2 id="-rlhf-as-dialogical-learning"><a class="anchor" href="#-rlhf-as-dialogical-learning">🤝 RLHF as Dialogical Learning</a></h2>
<p>Human feedback is not just “correction” — it’s conversation. RLHF introduces <strong>human intent into the learning loop</strong>, which makes the agent a <em>collaborative learner</em>, not a reward-maximizer.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="-conclusion"><a class="anchor" href="#-conclusion">🧠 Conclusion</a></h2>
<p>RLHF isn’t a patch. It’s a philosophical shift:<br>
From <strong>solving</strong> the reward function → to <strong>evolving</strong> through feedback.</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Perhaps intelligence is not about maximizing — but <strong>negotiating</strong>.</p>
