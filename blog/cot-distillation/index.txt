13:T502,<h2 id="-background"><a class="anchor" href="#-background">🧩 Background</a></h2>
<p>Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting enables powerful multi-step reasoning in LLMs. But transferring this ability into <strong>small language models (SLMs)</strong> remains a major challenge.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="-experiment-design"><a class="anchor" href="#-experiment-design">🧪 Experiment Design</a></h2>
<p>We study three axes:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Granularity</strong> of intermediate steps</li>
<li><strong>Supervision format</strong>: explanation-only vs answer+reasoning</li>
<li><strong>Teacher model</strong>: GPT-3.5 vs GPT-4</li>
</ol>
<p>7 datasets are used: GSM8K, SVAMP, DROP, etc.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="-key-findings"><a class="anchor" href="#-key-findings">📈 Key Findings</a></h2>
<ul>
<li>Fine-grained step supervision leads to <strong>more generalizable reasoning</strong></li>
<li>Answer-only distillation fails to transfer reasoning skills</li>
<li>GPT-4 outputs yield more structured logic chains than GPT-3.5</li>
</ul>
<hr>
<h2 id="-takeaway"><a class="anchor" href="#-takeaway">💡 Takeaway</a></h2>
<p>Distillation is not copying — it’s <strong>translation</strong>.</p>
<blockquote>
<p>“The way we teach reasoning determines what reasoning emerges.”</p>
